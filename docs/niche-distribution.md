# Distribution economics for niche apps

Generalized from real launch work in a market of a few thousand buyers a year.

## Compute the ceiling before spending anything

Regulators, licensing bodies, and industry associations publish volume numbers.
Unique annual buyers × realistic penetration × net price is the ceiling; know
it before allocating budget. Two consequences follow in small markets:

- Budget recovery is cheap (a handful of one-time purchases), so the risk of
  spending is low — but the ceiling caps what spending can ever return.
- Interest-based social ads structurally cannot pay: the audience is too small
  a fraction of any targetable interest group. Search ads on exact
  intent-bearing terms are the only paid channel that reaches a buyer at the
  moment of need.

## Store-page conversion before traffic

- **The app-name field outranks the keyword field.** A brand-only invented name
  carries zero search terms while competitors spend the name field on them.
  `Brand: Category Term` is a metadata edit — free, no build required. Spend
  the subtitle on the promise, not on keywords the name already covers.
- **Zero ratings is a conversion blocker ahead of any traffic problem.** Seed
  honest reviews by giving real prospective users free access — through
  instructors, cohorts, and practitioners. Ask for honest reviews, never for
  good ones.
- **Know which free-access mechanism your product actually needs.** App promo
  codes (a fixed allotment per version) only waive the *download* price, so
  they do nothing for a free app whose value sits behind an in-app purchase.
  Apple retired in-app-purchase promo codes on 26 March 2026 and replaced them
  with **offer codes**, which now cover consumables, non-consumables, and
  non-renewing subscriptions as well as auto-renewable ones. Offer codes live
  with the product (Monetization → In-App Purchases → the product), not on the
  promo-codes page, and are defined as a discount against a price point — a
  free offer is easiest to configure in the web console. Redemption is
  unchanged for the recipient ("Redeem Gift Card or Code"), so outreach copy
  does not change.
- **Rating prompts are a scarce resource.** The OS shows the system rating
  prompt roughly three times a year no matter how often it is requested. Gate
  it: only after the user succeeds at a meaningful milestone, only on a repeat
  success (not a first-session fluke), and at most once per app version. Never
  on launch, never mid-task.
- Price against the offline alternative the buyer already pays for (a course,
  a workshop, an hour of professional time), never against the cheapest app in
  the category.

## Channels, ranked by concentration

In a small market the question is where buyers concentrate, not where audiences
are large:

1. **Operators of the offline alternative** (course instructors, training
   chapters): their students are pre-qualified buyers. Offer the product free
   to the instructor and the cohort as a complement to the class, not a
   competitor. One instructor distributing codes beats the entire ad budget.
2. **Institutions feeding the pipeline** (schools, apprenticeship programs) —
   same logic, longer cycle.
3. **Practitioner communities** — these punish marketing and reward
   usefulness. Post the genuinely useful analysis (with sources) once, disclose
   ownership plainly, and let the product be a footnote. Read each community's
   self-promotion rules first; a ban costs the channel permanently.
4. **Exact-match search ads** on intent terms nobody else bids on — capped
   daily, killed per-keyword on cost-per-install, and only after the store
   page has ratings on it.

## Spend gates

Do not commit the full budget up front. Fund the free channels first, hold a
reserve, and spend it only on whichever channel demonstrably returned.
